## 1. Done when
Row 7's tests, word for word, for what this slice builds: After 20 flights, GPU resources are at their baseline and the JS heap is within 10%; 60 fps while travelling on a mid-range phone profile; with reduced motion, flights become crossfades and nothing orbits; every place stays reachable with the 3D off; on a phone, nothing of the 3D loads before "View in space"; after a minute with no input the scene asks at most 10 frames a second, and none when hidden or covered; the Auto probe drops to Low above 20 ms and remembers it; "Reduce animations" freezes motion; the bench holds 60 fps with the CPU slowed 4×.
Added: every screen of the frames named per branch is compared with them by the design-reviewer, and its geometry held by a test (decision of 2026-10-31); the cosmos draws the chain's realms, each planet seeded by its path (`sha256("gnoworld/planet/v1" ‖ path)`, ADR-0002 §3); the status ring is the tilted orbit with its moon, never the flat ring (decision of 2026-10-31); the scene runs under the page's real CSP with no violation; the first screen's JS budget is unchanged.
Added on 2026-11-01: a world is a product, its realms under it, and a namespace is a star; a hub with no realm is a world; the Earth, the general chat, always comes first; a world evolves with its use through two gauges, a vitality that remembers and the people present, and each class shows each stage its own way; popularity shows as matter, never as brightness; signs always mean real data, and the scenery is generative art that never imitates a sign; the arrival looks like frame f0004: near-black space, warm suns, real bodies, eight labels at most, the most popular and a few new ones, never an address; visual quality and animation are the video's at the very least, small details included (Alexis): it is the floor, never the ceiling, and the style is spectacular (Alexis); its quantities do not bind: everything holds at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 worlds.
Added by his last questions, the same day: worlds sit in a thick disc with clusters above and below it; the mood of space is a smooth field computed from a position, with no list, no count, no border and nothing stored; each system is a postcard, its star, nebula, rings and debris from its address, with a good arrival angle; the whole stays coherent: deep black, one light a system, one image grade, a palette from real space photography, the same labels.

## 4. Cost
Per frame, bounded by the distance ladder, never by the universe (ADR-0002 §3): planets, dust and moons instanced, static meshes merged per material, labels pooled and moved, their text changed 10 times a second at most. Bench (CPU slowed 4×) at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 realms: draw calls and frame time flat past the cap, with the draw bound recorded as a budget. Placement and planet parameters O(1) per realm, in a worker. Weight, measured today with esbuild 0.28.2, a bare scene, minified, gzip -9: three 0.187.0's WebGL renderer 133,691 B; its `three/webgpu` entry (the renderer with its WebGL2 fallback, ADR-0001 §12) 221,519 B. The first screen's cap is 173 KiB (177,152 B) and 7a must not move it, so three is a lazy chunk. The Earth's two textures at 4096 × 2048, WebP, take about 89 MB of GPU memory with their mipmaps, computed (4 B a pixel, a third more), not measured; the Low tier halves each side. The gate's price: `ALWAYS` in `scripts/harness_mutate.py` keys every verdict on `pnpm-lock.yaml`, so an install voids all 1,141: the next `--full` judges them all, about 6 h at the 3 a minute measured on 2026-11-01. So every dependency of this slice goes in at once, in one install.

## 5. Scale
Onyx today: 743 realms, 504 worlds, 91 namespaces, 390 `/p/` packages, 8 new realms in 7.5 hours (`.claude/tmp/slice-7/reports/onyx-realms.md`). What is drawn depends on distance and constant density alone; a world is a point from afar, and never keeps a fixed size on screen as you zoom out (the mock-up's pile-up, 2026-11-01). The first limit is the server's reads: one call per package, once; then the GPU memory of textures, a small reused set of maps warped and recoloured by the seed, released after five minutes covered. Past 10,000 worlds the next step is ADR-0002 §3's spatial tiles.
The scenery has no count: the mood and the space cells are computed from a position, so the sky costs the same at any size and only the worlds, which are data, grow. Positions on a GPU are 32-bit floats and lose precision far from the centre, so the scene is recentred on the camera; 7a tests it at a far coordinate.
